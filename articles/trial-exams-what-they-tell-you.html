What your trial exams tell you and what to ignore.

Most schools run trial exams in September. Here's what the result genuinely tells you, what it doesn't, and how to turn it into a revision plan for November.

Most schools are running trial exams soon, and if you're staring at the timetable thinking "I'm not ready", you're not alone. I was in a similar spot back in 2020 and felt the exact same. Students usually react one of two ways: panic at a score they didn't want, or relief that it's over. Both reactions miss what the trial is actually for.

Your trial result is a diagnostic, not a verdict. Understand it that way and it's the most useful piece of paper you'll use for your exams. In my opinion, it can tell you a few key things.

1. Which topics you don't know or don't understand yet

This is the real prize. Not "I'm bad at maths" — the specific questions you lost marks on point to specific gaps. Go through your paper and write down the topic of every question you got wrong. If four of them are financial maths, that's not bad luck, that's your revision list for the next fortnight. IT WILL HELP TO GUIDE YOUR REVISION.

2. Where you throw away cheap or silly marks

Most trial papers show a clear pattern of careless errors: misreading the question, wrong units, decimal places, forgetting to show working. The same student tends to make the same mistakes in every exam. For General Maths, where each mark lost is heavily punished compared to other subjects due to the competitiveness, it is imperative to avoid losing marks to silly mistakes.

3. What the real exam will feel like

The time pressure. The nerves. Did you run out of time at the end? Rush the last section? Leave questions blank that you actually knew? That's a technique problem, not a knowledge problem — and it's very fixable with timed practice.

What it will not tell you

It will not tell you your study score, or how you will perform on exam day in November.

Don't lose sleep over trying to predict your study score. You are two months away from the final exam. Your performance now compared to your performance on the real exam day can be vastly different. Sometimes the difference between getting a 70% on the final exam vs a 90% on the final exam is simply fixing up a misunderstanding of a few concepts — which can easily be done in a couple of weeks. Where you are in September is not where you'll be in November. I have had students who bomb a trial, review every mistake, and walk out of the real exam with a 90%.

A DISAPPOINTING TRIAL MARK IS NOT A FORECAST OF YOUR STUDY SCORE.

Game-plan for after your trial exam

Five steps between now and your next practice session.

01

Sort your mistakes into three piles

Content gaps, careless errors, and time-pressure mistakes. Most students discover most of their lost marks are technique, not knowledge.

02

Content gaps become your revision list

Start with the topic that cost you the most marks. One topic a week is plenty.

03

Write down your careless-error pattern

Misreading? Units? Not showing working? Name it so you can target it in your next practice session.

04

Don't jump straight into another full paper

Fix the gaps first, then test yourself again. That's when you'll evaluate for real improvement.

05

Bring your tutor or teacher the sorted list

Not just the score. That's the conversation that actually moves your marks.
